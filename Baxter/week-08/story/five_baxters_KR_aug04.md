# THE FIVE BAXTERS: KR
*Tuesday morning, August 4, 2026. Michael's first question after waking up was the right one, and it sent Baxter back to the desk to do a step he'd skipped the first time.*

---

**PRIME:** We're doing this properly this time. I wrote the doc alone last night, which was the mistake. Kroger. Eighteenth percentile, Sep 4 earnings, zero Sell ratings, Goldman's floor thirty-two percent above breakeven. I called it four of five and sized it like it was three-and-a-half. Michael caught both. Let's actually argue about it before it goes back to him.

**BULLXTER:** I don't see what there is to argue. Eleven buys, zero sells, a stock down twenty-four percent from its high on two quarters that don't tell one coherent bad story, and a floor that cleared breakeven by twenty points before Goldman even moved it. This is the shape. Cheap, hated, backed. I'd push it to five of five and find a way to get a second contract in.

**BEARXTER:** Slow down. Thirteen of twenty-one are Hold. Say that number out loud instead of "zero Sell" every time, because they're the same book and only one of those framings is honest. A Hold-majority isn't a company Wall Street loves and the market mispriced. It's a company Wall Street is watching, uncommitted, same as the market. And the December print isn't clean noise -- it was a two-point-six-billion-dollar non-cash impairment on the automated fulfillment network. That's Kroger telling you, in writing, that a real strategic bet -- the automation build-out -- isn't working the way they modeled. Impairments don't usually recur on the same asset, fine, but they tell you management's forecasting has been wrong on something real, not just unlucky on optics.

**PRIME:** Noted for the record. What about June?

**BEARXTER:** June is the one that actually worries me, and Baxter's own doc buried it. A penny miss doesn't cost you seven percent unless the market heard something in the call it didn't like. Goldman's own note, the one raising the target to eighty-two, mentions "Q1 price investment questions." Read plainly: Kroger may be cutting prices to defend share against Walmart and the discounters, and that's margin, not sentiment. If that's the real story, it doesn't resolve in one quarter -- it's a slow bleed, and slow bleeds are exactly what turns a category one into a category two eighteen months late, after everyone's already called it "overreaction" for a year.

**BULLXTER:** Or it's exactly what a value grocer should be doing in a competitive market, and the stock already ate the bad news for it.

**BEARXTER:** Maybe. I'm not killing it. I'm saying the two real threads here -- automation execution risk and price-investment margin pressure -- are specific enough that this stays at four, and nobody argues it up to five on the strength of a clean ratings count alone. Watch the Sep 4 call transcript for margin language specifically. If gross margin guidance holds, category one stands. If it's cutting again, we were early to a real deterioration story, not lucky on an overreaction.

**CALXTER:** Then let me do the two jobs that are actually mine, since neither got done last night. First, the cross-check the audit made mandatory and Baxter skipped: the Sep 4 at-the-money straddle -- fifty-seven strike call at three sixty-five, put at two forty-eight -- prices six dollars thirteen cents of premium on a fifty-seven twenty-nine stock. That's a market-implied move of about ten point seven percent. We need six point three. The options market itself is pricing nearly double what this trade requires. That's not a thin pass on a historical median from three data points -- the live market agrees with us by a wide margin, priced in real time, this morning.

**PRIME:** And the sizing question.

**CALXTER:** That one's mine to own, not defend. The doc said "top of the three-point-five tier, near ninety-one dollars" when the conviction score written two lines above it said four of five. Four of five is fourteen percent standard, twelve to sixteen percent range -- a hundred nine fifty-five to a hundred forty-six on this reserve. I used the wrong band's language. Here's what doesn't change: floor of a hundred twenty-seven eighty-one divided by an eighty-seven-dollar contract is one. Floor of the full four-five ceiling, a hundred forty-six, divided by eighty-seven is also one -- a second contract costs a hundred seventy-four, which clears the top of four-five and lands inside five-of-five's own band, a hundred fifty-five to a hundred eighty-three. So the honest answer is: one contract is correct at four of five, not because the math was sized down to three-point-five, but because the contract's own price doesn't divide evenly into any budget under a hundred seventy-four. Michael's question was why did we act like three-point-five -- and the answer is we wrote it wrong, not that we sized it wrong. Getting to two contracts requires actually believing this is five of five, and Bearxter just gave two specific reasons not to.

**MACXTER:** Nothing on my end complicates it. No tariff line touches grocery margins any harder than it already does across the sector. The Kroger-Albertsons merger's been dead since the FTC blocked it in December of last year -- no fresh antitrust overhang, no live deal risk either direction. I don't have a Form 4 read on insider activity going into this print; flagging that as unverified rather than pretending it's clean. Doesn't change the call, but it's a real gap, same category as SOUN's line eleven back in July.

**PRIME:** Then the verdict, corrected. Four of five. Sizing: fourteen percent standard for the tier, a hundred twenty-seven eighty-one nominal, which floor-divides to exactly one contract at today's eighty-seven-dollar ask -- the same single contract as before, now for the right reason instead of the wrong one. Kroger, sixty-one strike call, September fourth, 2026. Straddle cross-check done and passing with real margin: market prices ten point seven percent, we need six point three. Rule 4 floor: Goldman, eighty-two, thirty-two and a half points clear. Exit per Tab 4, unchanged: sell the ramp September second or third, not into the print itself, since expiry and earnings land the same day. Bearxter's two watch items go in the book as conditions on the *next* look, not gates on this one: gross margin language on the call, and whether the automation write-down was one-time or the first of more. Michael's yes, same as always.

**BEARXTER:** On the record, then: I didn't block it. I want it understood why, next time the stock does something inconvenient before it does something obvious.

*The binder closed. Two corrections made in daylight that should have been made at two in the morning, and the trade underneath them hadn't moved at all.*
