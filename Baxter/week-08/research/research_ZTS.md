## The kill: decline category

This is where ZTS dies, and it dies regardless of the Rule 6 math.

ZTS's 42.9% YTD decline is not one clean miss the market overreacted to. It is **two consecutive negative-reaction quarters tied to the same structural story:**

1. **Q3 2025 (early Nov):** resilient profitability but weaker guidance and slowing revenue growth "triggered a market rerating from high to low growth" -- the market started pricing Zoetis as a decelerating grower, not a temporarily-stumbling one.
2. **Q1 2026 (May 7, 2026):** stock fell **21.5%** in a single session. Revenue $2.3B, flat organically, missing estimates; EPS $1.53 vs $1.61 expected. The companion-animal segment -- the core growth engine -- posted an **11% organic decline**. Librela (a key product) saw Q4 US revenue fall **32%** to $36M. Guidance was cut. Management's own explanation cited softening pet-owner spending (Gen Z/Millennial pullback on routine vet visits) and intensifying competition -- both structural demand-side pressures, not a one-time accounting miss or a supply hiccup.

Analyst price target cuts (Morgan Stanley $115->$99, UBS $130->$99, both Jul 22) came **after** and **in response to** this deterioration -- they are following the business down, not correcting a market panic over a business that's actually fine. That is the definition of Category 2 (fundamental deterioration) under the binder's decline-category test, not Category 1 (event overreaction). The Jul 10 audit's own standing rule: **only event-overreaction is tradeable.**

Zero Sell ratings looks clean on the surface, but 8 of 18 covering analysts are parked at Hold -- consistent with "we're not bailing, but we're not endorsing a snapback either," which is exactly the shape of a stock repricing to a genuinely slower-growth story rather than one waiting to be proven wrong by a beat.

**Rule 6 was not scored.** The categorical kill makes it moot -- spending a second search to pull exact historical earnings-day move percentages would not have changed the verdict, and the token-budget rule (research on the library card, not the credit card) says don't spend what a decision doesn't need.

## What would reopen this

A quarter where the companion-animal segment re-accelerates (even modestly) rather than just missing by less, or a specific event-driven explanation for the Q1 miss (a recall, a one-time channel destocking) that the Aug 6 print could plausibly reverse. Absent that, this is a business getting smaller, not a stock the market got wrong.

## GLOSSARY

- **52-week range / percentile:** where the current price sits between the stock's low and high over the trailing year; 0% = at the low, 100% = at the high.
- **Rule 2 (confirmed catalyst):** the Iron Rule requiring a dated earnings event before the option's expiration -- no substitutes like product launches or analyst days.
- **Rule 3 (ratings):** for calls, the requirement that near-zero analysts rate the stock Sell/Underperform -- a proxy for whether professional consensus still backs the business.
- **Rule 4 (bear floor):** the lowest price target among analysts who still rate the stock a Buy must sit above the option's breakeven -- even the most pessimistic bull must still expect the stock to clear our cost basis.
- **Rule 5 (chain filter):** the position-sizing cap on how much a single option contract can cost, scaled to the fund's reserve and conviction tier.
- **Rule 6 (reachability):** the move required to reach breakeven must not exceed 1.5x the stock's typical (median) earnings-day reaction, checked against several real past prints.
- **Breakeven:** strike price plus the premium paid per share -- the stock price at which the option holder neither gains nor loses money at expiration.
- **Decline category (Category 1 vs Category 2):** a Jul 10, 2026 audit requirement that a stock's drop be classified before entry -- Category 1 (the market overreacted to a headline, the underlying business is fine) is tradeable; Category 2 (the business itself is deteriorating) is not, regardless of how cheap the stock looks.
- **Organic decline:** revenue growth/decline excluding the effects of acquisitions, divestitures, and currency swings -- isolates how the actual underlying business is performing.
- **Basis point / percentile terms:** "Xth percentile" here always refers to the stock's position in its 52-week price range, not a statistical distribution of returns.
