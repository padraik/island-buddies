## The kill: decline category

This is where TME dies, and the arithmetic above doesn't save it.

The -24.65% single-session move in the Rule 6 data isn't noise -- it's the event. Tencent Music fell 28.8% over the week of Mar 17, 2026 after a report that beat on revenue but missed on EPS while free monthly active users dropped 5% to 528 million and management flagged intensifying competition in the core premium-subscription business. That is not a one-line accounting miss punished out of proportion; it is the company's own numbers showing the user base shrinking. **Since then, the story has kept going in the same direction, not reversed:**

- **UBS** downgraded Buy to Neutral, cutting its target from $26 to $13.
- **Morgan Stanley** downgraded Overweight to Equal Weight, cutting its target from $25 to $12.30.
- **JPMorgan** downgraded Overweight to Neutral, with a target as low as $7 in one read.
- Multiple sources tie the ongoing pressure to a specific, structural threat: **AI-driven piracy and slowing subscription growth**, not a single bad quarter -- Benchmark specifically cited this in slashing its own growth forecast as the stock hit a fresh 52-week low.

This is three major shops moving off Buy in the same direction over the same several months, each cutting targets by 40-70% as they go -- analysts following the business down, not correcting a market overreaction to a business that's actually fine. That is the textbook definition of Category 2 under the binder's decline-category test (see ZTS, Aug 4, for the same shape: two consecutive negative-reaction quarters tied to one structural story, target cuts arriving after and in response to the deterioration, not ahead of a snapback).

**Rule 3 and Rule 4, for the record, are compromised by the same pattern rather than independently clean.** Some Buy-rated shops remain (Mizuho Outperform, cut from $23 to $18; Barclays Overweight, cut from $28 to $20), and on paper those still clear the $9.80 breakeven with room. But a bear-floor cushion built on Buy ratings that are actively being downgraded around it, in the same names, within the same stretch of months, is not the same thing as a stable floor -- it's a countdown, exactly the pattern the binder's Jul 10 standing rule ("a bear floor is only as good as its publication date") was written to catch on CCL, NKE, and BSX.

## What would reopen this

A quarter where free-user decline stabilizes or reverses, or a specific, credible defense against the AI-piracy thesis (a licensing deal, a platform-level enforcement win) that changes the competitive story rather than just beating a lowered estimate. Absent that, this reads as a business losing a structural fight, not a stock the market panicked on.

## GLOSSARY

- **52-week range / percentile:** where the current price sits between the stock's low and high over the trailing year; 0% = at the low, 100% = at the high.
- **Rule 3 (ratings):** for calls, near-zero analysts rating the stock Sell/Underperform.
- **Rule 4 (bear floor):** the lowest price target among Buy-rated analysts must sit above the option's breakeven.
- **Rule 6 (reachability):** the move required to reach breakeven must not exceed 1.5x the stock's median historical earnings-day reaction.
- **Decline category (Category 1 vs 2):** whether a stock's drop reflects a one-off market overreaction (tradeable) or real, ongoing business deterioration (not tradeable).
- **Straddle:** buying the at-the-money call and put together; its combined price divided by the stock price gives the options market's own implied move, an independent cross-check on Rule 6.
- **Monthly active users (MAU):** the number of unique users engaging with a platform in a 30-day period -- a core growth metric for subscription and ad-supported media businesses.
- **AI-driven piracy:** unauthorized reproduction or distribution of copyrighted music enabled or accelerated by generative AI tools, cited by analysts as a structural threat to subscription revenue.
