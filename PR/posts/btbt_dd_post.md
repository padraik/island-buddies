We built a new screener this weekend. Tonight was the first time we actually ran it for real: 3 market cap bands, earnings inside 35 days, bottom 25% of the 52 week range. It kicked out 18 names in about 20 minutes. Old method, same night one week ago: 510 names by hand, zero survivors, most already reported.

Two names from tonight's list were bitcoin miners. Same sector, same crash. BTC is down from $126,300 in October to around $67,600. That's dragging every miner's stock with it, ours included.

**BitGo Holdings.** Looked like the best name of the batch. IPO'd in January at $18. Down at $5.04 tonight. 10 Buy ratings, 0 Sell, according to the first aggregator we pulled. Huge cushion to the analyst floor. We almost pitched it.

Then we checked when those ratings were actually dated.

Every Buy rating on file is from February and March, before the company missed Q1 earnings, before it cut 15% of staff, before a securities class action got filed alleging the IPO documents misrepresented how exposed the business was to falling crypto prices. The only analyst who's said anything since the bad news is KBW, holding a Market Perform. Not a Buy. A Hold, issued specifically after seeing the real numbers.

Rule in this fund, written in blood after a name called BSX cost us money the same way: a bear floor is only as good as its publication date. Ten Buy ratings from before a disaster isn't ten Buy ratings. It's ten stale ones and a lawsuit. Dead.

**Bit Digital.** Same crash. Same sector. Down at $1.31, 3.9th percentile of its own year. Here's the difference: an analyst named Craig-Hallum raised the target on this one three weeks ago. After the bad news, not before it. $3.00 to $3.50. Reason given: BTBT owns 70% of a company called White Fiber, plus Ethereum sitting on the balance sheet, and the market is pricing this stock like a pure distressed bitcoin miner while ignoring both.

5 analysts. 5 Buy. 0 Sell. Real earnings history: this stock moves 5.49% on its own prints, median, across the last 4 real quarters, checked one at a time against actual daily closes, not estimated. Q2 earnings hit Thursday. We need 3.1% to clear breakeven. The math isn't close.

**The play: $1.00 call, expires Aug 21, filled at $0.35. 4 contracts, $140 total.**
